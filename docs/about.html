Methods & data

Parish areas

Parishes have no published territorial boundaries here, so each parish's area is built from 2020-definition census tracts in the ten counties of the Archdiocese (Dodge, Fond du Lac, Kenosha, Milwaukee, Ozaukee, Racine, Sheboygan, Walworth, Washington, Waukesha). Two definitions can be switched on every page:

  • Nearest-parish tracts. Every tract is assigned to the parish whose closest active church/worship site is nearest to the tract's centre (straight-line distance). A tract that physically contains a parish site always belongs to that parish. The only overlap occurs when sites of two or more parishes are in the same tract — that tract then counts for all of them. Multi-site parishes use whichever of their sites is closest. Parishes marked boundary = no (shrines, campus ministry, personal/ethnic parishes, Eastern Catholic churches) do not take territory; their profile shows only the tract(s) their sites are in.
  • Tracts within X miles. All tracts whose centre lies within the chosen radius of any active site of the parish (plus the tract containing the site). Areas overlap freely; the radius can be changed on the page.

The tract “centre” is the area centroid, moved inside the tract when the shape is irregular. Archdiocese totals are always computed from all tracts once, so overlapping parish areas never double-count.

Combining ACS estimates across tracts

  • Counts are summed. Margins of error are combined as the square root of the sum of squared tract MOEs (Census Bureau approximation), shown on hover in profiles.
  • Percentages are recomputed exactly as Σ numerator ÷ Σ denominator when the build script can find the numerator and denominator count columns in the same table (it checks this against the published tract percentages). Otherwise they are averaged, weighted by the table's universe count (e.g. workers 16+), which equals the exact result when the universe is right.
  • Medians and means (median income, mean travel time…) are weighted averages of tract values. For medians this is an approximation and is labelled “approx.”.
  • Custom indicators (data/derived_variables.csv) are ratios of sums, e.g. people speaking Spanish at home ÷ population 5+. They are exact for any area and may combine columns from different tables.

Every column's rule is listed in docs/data/acs/<TABLE>.report.txt after a build, and any rule can be overridden in data/acs_overrides.csv.

ACS 5-year tract estimates have sizeable margins of error, especially for small areas and small groups. Treat differences of a few points between parishes with care.

Sources

  • U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 5-year estimates (tables listed on the overview's variable picker).
  • Census tract boundaries: U.S. Census Bureau cartographic boundary files (1:500,000, 2022 vintage).
  • Parish sites: archmil.org parish finder, curated in data/parishes.csv and data/locations.csv.
  • Basemap © OpenStreetMap contributors, © CARTO.

Maintaining the site

Add more ACS data

  1. On data.census.gov, open any table (Subject “S…”, Data Profile “DP…”, or Detailed “B…/C…”), set Geography to Census Tract → Wisconsin → all ten archdiocese counties, choose the 5-year dataset, and download it as CSV (ZIP).
  2. Put the ZIP, unmodified, in data/acs/. Several downloads of the same table (e.g. one per county) are merged automatically.
  3. Run python3 scripts/build.py (or just push to GitHub — the workflow rebuilds the site).

Correct or categorise parishes

  • Use Edit parishes to move sites, merge/split parishes, set categories, regions/deaneries and tags, and mark sites inactive. Then download both CSVs into data/ and rebuild — or edit the CSVs directly in Excel.
  • When archmil.org changes, save the parish-finder HTML to data/source/archmil_places.html and run python3 scripts/import_places.py. New sites are appended (marked REVIEW); your corrections are never overwritten; differences are listed in data/source/import_report.txt.
